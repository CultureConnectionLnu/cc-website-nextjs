# Code of Conduct for Culture Connection

## 1. Purpose

With this Code of Conduct, Culture Connection seeks to strengthen social safety and inclusivity in our association by providing clear rules on what is considered unacceptable behaviour and what to do when somebody breaks these rules.

## 2. Persons to whom this Code of Conduct applies

This Code applies to any person participating in any Culture Connection activities, whether they are a guest, regular member, volunteer, committee member or board member (hereafter “attendee”). The Code covers behaviour at:

- All events hosted by Culture Connection
- All Culture Connection (board) meetings
- All external representation of Culture Connection

Hereafter, the word “events” refers to all the aforementioned Culture Connection activities.

## 3. Alcohol and drugs policy

Culture Connection is an alcohol free association. This means that consumption of alcohol at Culture Connection events is prohibited. Furthermore, attending Culture Connection events while under the influence of alcohol is also prohibited. If a person is under the influence of alcohol, the person has consumed some intoxicating beverage in such a quantity, whether small or great, to cause an adverse effect on their actions, reactions, conduct, movement or mental processes, or impairs their reactions to an appreciable degree. This same policy is extended to all substances that are considered drugs or narcotics by the Swedish government.

## 4. Behaviour

All attendees are expected to act in accordance with the social conduct set out by this Code. The following section will set out what behaviour is considered intolerable and how Culture Connection defines this behaviour.

### 4.1 Discrimination

Any unfair or unequal treatment of individuals or groups based on specific characteristics such as sex, gender identity or gender expression, sexual orientation, ethnicity, religion or other belief, disability, socio-economic background, or age.

### 4.2 Sexual harassment

Behaviour of a sexual nature that violates someone’s dignity. In addition to comments and words, this may include, for example, unwanted touching or intrusive looks. It may also involve unwelcome compliments, invitations, or insinuations. Whether behaviour is considered sexual harassment is independent from the intentions of the person exhibiting the behaviour.

### 4.3 Threats

May be verbal or written warnings of physical violence or damage, or may occur through body language, gestures, presence at a specific place, or through an apparently harmless comment.

### 4.4 Violence

Aggressive actions that lead to physical or psychological harm to another person, for example pushing, tearing, or restraining.

### 4.5 Abusive treatment

Reprehensible or negative actions directed at one or more students that may result in those exposed being excluded from the community. If repeated and ongoing over a longer period, it may develop into bullying. Bullying is defined as recurring negative actions over a longer period of time directed at an individual or a group.

## 5. What to do?

### 5.1 Contact

If you are the victim of misconduct or witnessed misconduct against others this can be reported to the board via the contact form on the website. You can also always approach any Culture Connection board member during events. Make sure to write down all incidents, save any text, emails, or other correspondence with the person whose misconduct you are reporting. In cases of extreme or repeated misconduct, do not try to resolve the issue yourself. The board is here to assist and support you wherever necessary. If the incident involves a Culture Connection board member you should contact the Linnaeus Union. Follow their Policy for member incidents document that can be found on linnek.se/documents. In any situation where any person’s health or safety is in acute danger, call 112.

### 5.2 Privacy

When you report an incident(s) to the board your identity, the identities of the people involved, and details of the incident(s) are considered sensitive information and will not be shared with anybody else in the association without explicit permission.

### 5.3 Disciplinary actions

When an incident is reported, the board will perform an inquiry to determine the details of the incident. Afterwards, the board will decide what steps should be taken. Depending on the severity of the incident the following actions could be taken:

- The offender can be demanded to leave from a particular event
- The offender can have their role as board or committee member revoked
- The offender can have their membership status removed and banned from future
  events
- The victim can be connected to any necessary authorities such as the police, the
  student health centre, etc.
